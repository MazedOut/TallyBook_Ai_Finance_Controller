## 1. The Problem Space

Reconciliation — matching bank transactions against internal ledgers, invoices, and payments — remains one of the most manual, error-prone processes in finance operations, despite decades of broader accounting digitization. It is still commonly done via spreadsheets or partial ERP matching, with humans manually chasing down mismatches, duplicates, and unmatched entries at month-end close.

## 2. Market Size & Growth (Reconciliation Software)

Multiple independent market research firms converge on the same growth story, even where absolute figures vary by methodology:

- The global reconciliation software market is projected to grow from $2.8 billion in 2025 to $3.32 billion in 2026, a CAGR of 18.6%, reaching an estimated $6.43 billion by 2030 (Research and Markets, 2026).
- A separate estimate values the account reconciliation software market at $2.79 billion in 2026, growing to $7.11 billion by 2033 at a 14.3% CAGR — with the bank reconciliation segment specifically forecast to hold the largest functional share (44.3%) in 2026, due to its role in monitoring cash flow and capital position (Coherent Market Insights).
- A longer-horizon forecast projects the market reaching $15.52 billion by 2035, with bank & cash reconciliation holding the largest functional share (~27%) as of 2025 (Precedence Research).
- Regionally, Asia Pacific contributed roughly $0.79 billion in 2025 (34.1% global share) and is expected to post the highest CAGR of any region going forward, driven by startups adopting modernized reconciliation solutions (Fortune Business Insights).
- AI is explicitly named as the next phase of this market's evolution: AI-powered tools are increasingly used to match complex transactions across systems, detect anomalies, predict potential mismatches, and generate intelligent audit trails — shifting finance teams from repetitive manual, month-end crunches toward continuous reconciliation (Precedence Research).

**Takeaway:** this is a large, fast-growing category (mid-teens to high-teens CAGR across every estimate), and the market's own trajectory is already pointing toward AI-assisted matching and auditability — exactly the wedge TallyBook is built on.

## 3. The MSME Opportunity Specifically (India-first framing)

This is the more important number for our go-to-market, because it shows a market that is large, underserved, and already primed by adjacent digital adoption:

- India has over 63.4 million MSMEs, contributing roughly 30% of national GDP (Hisabkitab / Statista).
- Despite this scale, only 29% of tech-savvy MSMEs in India currently use accounting software at all, and 6% of MSMEs still rely on entirely manual methods with no software whatsoever (PayNearby MSME Digital Index 2024).
- Where digital tools have landed, adoption is mobile-first: smartphones are the preferred digital device for over 70% of micro-enterprise users, and UPI (43%) and Aadhaar banking (38%) are the most popular last-mile financial services among MSMEs, with WhatsApp/WhatsApp Business used by 97% of them.
- Digital adoption already proves it moves the needle on outcomes: 68% of MSMEs report business growth after adopting digital technology, and among broader SME software adopters, 57% report improved transaction accuracy and 49% report enhanced compliance monitoring after implementing automation.

**Takeaway:** the MSME segment is not resistant to digital tools — it has already demonstrated fast, mobile-first adoption of UPI, WhatsApp Business, and basic accounting software. The gap isn't willingness, it's that reconciliation-specific tooling hasn't been built for this segment yet.

## 4. Where Reconciliation Ranks as an Actual Pain Point

This is the most directly relevant data point for justifying product-market fit:

- A 2026 study based on over 2,400 product demonstrations of accounting automation tools among Indian SMEs found that bill/accounts-payable processing was the top-cited pain point (69.6% of businesses), followed by bank and credit card reconciliation (14%) and GST reconciliation (8.1%) (IBS Intelligence, 2026).
- Even with widespread digitization of payments and banking, many Indian SMEs still rely on manual workflows for invoice collection, data entry, reconciliation, and financial reporting.
- Notably, founders and business owners themselves — not just accountants — made up 37.3% of demo attendees in that study, and had the highest conversion-to-paying-customer rate at 10.7%, suggesting the buyer for this category is often the business owner directly, not exclusively a hired finance function.

**Takeaway:** reconciliation is a named top-3 pain point even in a market where payments and GST are already digitized — meaning the bottleneck isn't data availability, it's the matching/verification layer. This validates the "why now" framing of the buildathon track itself: verification capacity, not generation or data collection, is the actual constraint.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

- Enterprise incumbents are already moving in this direction: Oracle announced AI-driven account reconciliation capabilities enabling real-time transaction matching and anomaly detection in March 2026, and SAP integrated machine-learning-powered reconciliation automation into SAP S/4HANA Finance in February 2026 — both explicitly targeted at large enterprises (DataM Intelligence).
- Mid-market players are also active: Trintech expanded its Adra Suite in mid-2025 with cloud-native reconciliation capabilities aimed specifically at mid-sized enterprises, while Xero added AI-driven bank reconciliation automation aimed at small and medium-sized businesses in January 2025.
- Other named incumbents in the broader space include BlackLine, Broadridge Financial Solutions, Sage, Intuit, Rimilia, Fiserv, and AutoRek (KBV Research).

**Takeaway:** the enterprise and mid-market tiers already have credible AI-driven incumbents (Oracle, SAP, Trintech, Xero). The true underserved segment sits below that — small MSMEs and micro-businesses who are priced out of, or simply unaware of, enterprise reconciliation tooling, and are still relying on spreadsheets or nothing at all. This is TallyBook's opening.

## 6. Why Now (ties back to the buildathon track framing)

- The market itself is already moving toward continuous, AI-assisted reconciliation instead of manual month-end crunches.
- MSMEs have already proven fast adoption of other digital financial tools (UPI, WhatsApp Business, basic accounting software) — the receptivity exists.
- Reconciliation is a named, measurable top-3 pain point even after payments/GST digitization — this is not a hypothetical problem.
- Existing AI-driven reconciliation tools are being built for enterprise and mid-market, leaving a large, digitally-primed, currently underserved population of small businesses without an accessible, trustworthy option.
